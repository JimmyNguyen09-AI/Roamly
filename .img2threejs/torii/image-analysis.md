# Roamly torii image analysis

## Identification

- Stylized Shinto torii miniature, `object` domain, confidence 0.99.
- Intended use: real-time browser storytelling landmark with a centered pass-through opening.

## Form and silhouette

- Bilaterally symmetric architectural frame with two slightly tapered circular posts.
- Wide horizontal nuki beam, upper shimagi beam and strongly upturned kasagi cap.
- Two-tier stone plinths create a broad grounded footprint.

## Hierarchy

- Macro: centered root, left/right post assemblies, cross-beam assembly and left/right foundations.
- Meso: tapered post shafts, square head blocks, nuki, central gakuzuka support, shimagi, curved kasagi, cylindrical stone collars and square stone plinths.
- Micro: beam end-caps, shallow joinery overlaps, bevels, vermilion edge wear and stone relief.

## Spatial relationships

- Each post sockets into a stone collar above a square stepped plinth.
- The nuki passes through both posts and projects beyond them.
- Small tie blocks sit above the nuki near each post.
- Shimagi rests across the post heads; curved kasagi caps it and turns upward at both ends.
- Central gakuzuka hangs between upper beam and nuki.

## Materials

- Vermilion painted timber: dielectric, matte/satin, roughness about 0.62 with darker edge wear.
- Dark charcoal cap: dielectric painted timber, roughness about 0.72.
- Granite foundation: dielectric, roughness about 0.9 with non-uniform tonal speckle.

## Identity features

- Pronounced symmetric upward sweep of the black kasagi.
- Layered vermilion shimagi beneath the dark cap.
- Tapered round posts rather than rectangular pillars.
- Nuki beam projecting on both sides.
- Gakuzuka central support and small post-head joinery blocks.
- Two-tier stone bases with differing footprints.

## Unknowns

- Rear joinery, exact beam depth and back-face surface wear are hidden.
- Curvature is inferred from one three-quarter view; result is a stylized reconstruction rather than measured architecture.
